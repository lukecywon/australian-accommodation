Where Australia
Stays

From visitor nights and hotel locations to the economic footprint of accommodation.

Tourism is not one uniform market. Domestic and international visitors travel at different times, stay in different kinds of places, and support different parts of the economy. This visual story follows five questions: where tourists go, when they travel, where they stay, where hotels are located, and what accommodation means for the wider economy.

Charts draw on different source periods from 2018-2019 to June 2026. The reporting period and population are identified with each figure.

Chapter 01

The geography of a night away

Where tourists travel depends on the measure we choose. Total visitor nights show the size of each state's tourism market, while visitor nights relative to population reveal a different perspective. We begin with that contrast before looking at where accommodation spending is concentrated and how domestic travel compares with international travel.

Where a trip happens is only one part of the story. The next question is when and why people travel.

Chapter 02

Travel moves with the calendar

Tourism demand does not hold steady through the year. Recent quarters show different peaks and slowdowns across states, while reasons for travel help explain why those patterns vary. The next two charts examine timing without assuming that five quarters establish a long-term seasonal cycle.

The timing of travel matters. But a visitor night can be spent in a hotel, a rented property, or a home belonging to friends and family.

Chapter 03

Beyond the hotel room

Accommodation is more varied than a single hotel market. International visitors spend nights in commercial and private accommodation, and domestic visitors make different choices between capital-city and regional trips. These charts move from the national mix to the contrasts between places.

With accommodation types in view, we can look more closely at the hotels themselves and how international accommodation use has changed.

Chapter 04

Hotels on the map, choices in motion

Statistics by state summarise large areas. Individual hotel locations reveal a different geographic pattern: establishments cluster around particular places rather than spreading evenly across the map. Alongside that location snapshot, we compare how international visitor nights changed between accommodation types.

Visitor behaviour and the location of accommodation establishments lead into one final question: what does this activity mean for the wider economy?

Chapter 05

The wider economic footprint

Accommodation supports output, jobs and tourism spending, but different indicators tell different stories. We first compare state economic profiles, then trace which spending categories contributed to an annual change. The final chart broadens the lens by combining Tourism Research Australia spending with Australian Bureau of Statistics business counts.

What the patterns tell us

Australia's accommodation landscape changes depending on what we measure. Total demand is not the same as demand relative to population. A recorded hotel location is not a measure of occupancy. Spending, visitor nights and business counts describe different parts of tourism. Read together, and with their time periods and definitions visible, these charts tell a richer story than a single ranking could.

Sources & methodology

This Data Visualisation assignment combines Tourism Research Australia, Australian Bureau of Statistics and OpenStreetMap data. Figure subtitles identify reporting periods; axes and legends identify units. Publication and table references are listed below by figure.

Figure 3 combines Domestic Tourism Statistics Table 1c and International Visitor Survey Table 3. Figure 4 uses Domestic Tourism Statistics Table 1b; figure 5 uses Table 3b; figure 12 uses Table 4a.
  • Figures 2 and 11: Tourism Research Australia State Tourism Satellite Accounts, financial year 2024-2025. Figure 2 uses accommodation-services consumption from Table 9. Figure 11 uses output, employment, gross value added and consumption from Tables 5, 6, 7 and 9.
  • Figures 6 and 10: Tourism Research Australia International Visitor Survey, Table 4. Figure 6 uses the year ending June 2026; figure 10 compares the years ending June 2025 and June 2026.
  • Figures 7 and 8: Tourism Research Australia National Visitor Survey Overnight Accommodation Trends, year ending December 2024. Figure 7 includes four accommodation types with matched capital-city and regional values. Figure 8 covers New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia and Western Australia, with three selected accommodation categories.
  • Figure 9: OpenStreetMap contributors, hotel records from the supplied accommodation extract. The extraction date has not been confirmed. Coverage is incomplete and Other Territories are excluded. OpenStreetMap attribution and Open Database Licence.
  • Figure 13: Tourism Research Australia State Tourism Satellite Accounts 2024-2025, Table 4, and Australian Bureau of Statistics Counts of Australian Businesses, Table 1. Financial years 2018-2019 to 2024-2025; index base 2018-2019 = 100. Spending is nominal. Business counts combine archived and newer releases with a June 2022 revision. Spending and business counts cover different industry scopes.
